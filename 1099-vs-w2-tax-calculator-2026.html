Why 1099 and W-2 Taxes Are Different

W-2 Employee

Your employer withholds taxes and pays half your FICA (7.65%). You never see the employer half — but on a $75,000 salary it costs them ~$5,750 extra.

  • FICA: you pay 7.65%
  • Employer pays other 7.65%
  • Federal and state withheld automatically
  • Benefits (health, 401k) often included

1099 Contractor

You generally pay both shares of Social Security and Medicare taxes as self-employment tax. Deductible business expenses, half of regular SE tax, and a potential QBI deduction can affect the comparison.

  • Regular SE tax is generally 15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings, up to the Social Security wage base for the Social Security portion
  • Additional Medicare Tax may apply
  • Deductible half of regular SE tax reduces AGI
  • Eligible taxpayers may claim up to 20% QBI, subject to limitations
  • Deduct ordinary and necessary business expenses on Schedule C

Frequently Asked Questions

Do 1099 workers pay more taxes than W-2 employees?
Self-employed workers generally pay both shares of Social Security and Medicare taxes, while W-2 employees pay the employee share and their employer pays the employer share. Business deductions, benefits, filing details, and other income can change the overall comparison.
What is self-employment tax in 2026?
Regular SE tax is 12.4% Social Security tax on net earnings (up to the $184,500 wage base) plus 2.9% Medicare tax on all net earnings, generally after applying the 92.35% factor. An additional 0.9% Medicare tax may apply above $200,000 for single filers or $250,000 for joint filers. Half of regular SE tax is generally deductible for income-tax purposes.
What is the QBI deduction and who qualifies?
Eligible taxpayers may deduct up to 20% of qualified business income. Eligibility and limits depend on taxable income, filing status, business type, and other rules; the calculator uses a simplified assumption and may not reflect your allowed deduction.
Should I form an S-Corp to reduce SE tax?
An S corporation may affect employment taxes, but potential savings depend on reasonable compensation, business income, administrative costs, and compliance obligations. Consult a qualified tax professional before choosing an entity or tax election.
