✨ NEW 2026: Qualified Tips Deduction (Up to $25,000)

Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), Grubhub workers who receive tips can now deduct up to $25,000 in qualified tips from federal taxable income. Tips are still reported as income on your 1099, but you take an above-the-line deduction that reduces your AGI. Phase-out begins at $150,000 (single) / $300,000 (MFJ).

Pennsylvania taxes gig income under a 3.07% flat state tax, plus a local Earned Income Tax (EIT) set by your municipality or school district. You file Schedule C (or Schedule E for some rental income) and claim all standard deductions on top.

Frequently Asked Questions
What can Pennsylvania Grubhub drivers deduct in 2026?
All standard federal deductions (mileage, phone, platform fees, health insurance) plus anything specific to operating in Pennsylvania. See the full table above.
Do Pennsylvania gig workers owe local tax on top of the state rate?
Usually yes. Beyond the 3.07% flat state rate, most PA municipalities and school districts levy a local Earned Income Tax (EIT), commonly 1%-3.9% depending on where you live — Philadelphia's resident rate is among the highest in the state.
What happens if I miss a quarterly estimated tax payment?
The IRS charges an underpayment penalty equal to the federal short-term rate plus 3 percentage points — currently 7% annually for Q3 2026, compounded daily on the shortfall until it's paid.
What 1099 form does Grubhub send?
Grubhub issues Form 1099-K for platform payments processed above the reporting threshold, and Form 1099-NEC for bonuses, referrals, or other non-driving income.
