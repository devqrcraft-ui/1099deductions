1099 Tax Deductions List 2026 —
Ultimate Guide for U.S. Gig Workers

A Form 1099 reports certain payments, but does not by itself determine your worker classification or tax treatment. This guide reviews common potential business expenses for gig workers and freelancers in 2026 — whether you drive for DoorDash or Lyft, host on Airbnb, sell on Etsy, or freelance as a designer or software engineer. Eligibility, allocation, and reporting depend on your activity and circumstances; not every deduction applies to every worker. This page is educational only — always consult a CPA for your specific situation.

What is a 1099 worker?

A Form 1099 reports certain payments; receiving one does not by itself determine whether you are an employee, independent contractor, or have taxable business income. Many gig workers and freelancers report business income and eligible expenses on Schedule C, while rental income and other activities may follow different rules. Self-employment tax may apply to net earnings from self-employment. A deductible business expense generally reduces taxable profit, but it does not reduce tax dollar-for-dollar.

Mileage and vehicle expenses

For most 1099 workers, your car is one of the biggest tax deductions available. In 2026, the IRS standard mileage rate is 72.5¢ per business mile (Jan–Jun) or 76¢ (Jul–Dec). Every 1,000 business miles reduces your taxable income by $725. You must keep a mileage log separating business miles from personal trips — apps like MileIQ, Everlance, or Stride make this automatic.

If eligible, you may use the standard-mileage method or deduct the business portion of actual vehicle costs, subject to the applicable rules and method restrictions. Compare the methods using your records. Qualifying business parking and tolls may be deductible separately when allowed; personal costs are not business deductions.

Home office deduction

If you run your 1099 business from home, a dedicated workspace can unlock a valuable deduction. The simplified method lets you claim $5 per square foot up to 300 sq ft — a maximum of $1,500 per year. The space must be used regularly and exclusively for your business, not as a shared family room or bedroom.

Advanced filers can instead deduct a percentage of rent, mortgage interest, utilities, and homeowners insurance based on the share of the home used for work. Keep photos and a floor plan in case the IRS asks.

Phone, data, and internet

Almost every 1099 worker uses a phone and internet connection to earn income. You can deduct the business-use percentage of your cell phone plan, mobile data, and home internet bill. If you use your phone 70% for business, you can deduct 70% of your monthly bill. Document how you estimated your percentage.

Equipment, tools, and supplies

Equipment, tools, and supplies may be deductible to the extent they are ordinary, necessary, and used for business. Mixed personal and business use generally requires allocation. Depending on the asset and current rules, costs may be deducted, depreciated, or eligible for a Section 179 election; immediate expensing is not automatic.

Software, apps, and online services

Subscriptions you pay to run your business are fully deductible — bookkeeping apps, mileage trackers, design software, cloud storage, CRM systems, AI tools, and website hosting. If you only use part of a subscription for business, deduct just the business portion.

Health insurance for self-employed workers

If you meet the eligibility requirements, you may qualify for the self-employed health insurance deduction for eligible medical, dental, and long-term care premiums. The deduction is generally claimed on Form 1040 rather than Schedule C, is subject to earned-income and other limits, and generally is not available for months you were eligible for subsidized employer coverage.

Travel, meals, and lodging

Travel expenses may qualify when you travel away from your tax home for business and meet the applicable requirements. Business meals are generally subject to a 50% limit, with exceptions. Personal costs do not become deductible just because you check email during a trip.

Education, training, and professional services

Costs to maintain or improve skills in your current line of 1099 work are usually deductible: courses, certifications, conferences, coaching, and industry memberships. You can also deduct professional services — accountants, tax preparers, lawyers, and business consultants who support your self-employed income.

Self-employment tax deduction

Self-employment tax generally covers the Social Security and Medicare taxes on qualifying net earnings from self-employment, subject to applicable limits and rules. Eligible taxpayers may deduct the employer-equivalent portion when figuring adjusted gross income. This deduction does not reduce the self-employment tax itself.

Frequently asked questions about 1099 deductions

Do I need to receive a 1099 form to claim deductions?
No. You must report all self-employed income even without a 1099-NEC or 1099-K. Your right to claim deductions depends on whether the expense is ordinary and necessary for your business, not on whether you received a form.
Do small amounts below the 1099 reporting threshold still count as taxable income?
Yes. The reporting threshold ($2,000 for 2026 payments; $600 for 2025) only controls when a company must issue a 1099-NEC. The IRS expects you to report all income from gigs and clients, regardless of amount or whether a form was issued.
How long should I keep receipts and records?
The IRS generally recommends keeping business records for at least three years after you file. Keep mileage logs, invoices, bank statements, and digital receipts organized by year.
Can I deduct money I send to family as a business expense?
No. Personal gifts or support payments to family are not ordinary and necessary business expenses and cannot be deducted against your 1099 income.
What is the 2026 IRS standard mileage rate?
The 2026 IRS standard mileage rate is 72.5¢ per business mile (Jan–Jun) or 76¢ (Jul–Dec) for gig workers driving for DoorDash, Uber, Lyft, Instacart, Amazon Flex, and similar platforms.
